How does the model work?

What is a marketing mix model?

A regression on weekly history. It splits each week's sales into a base you would get anyway, seasons, holidays, events, outside forces like the economy and price, and the part each media channel drove.

How does it handle ads that keep working?

Carry-over. Part of each week's spend keeps working in the weeks after. TV and video carry over for weeks. Search mostly works the week it runs. The model finds each channel's rate from the data.

Why do the curves bend?

Saturation. The first dollars reach the easiest buyers. Each extra dollar reaches fewer new ones. Past the steep part of the curve, the next dollar returns less than the average. Budget moves follow the next dollar.

Where do the ranges come from?

The model refits itself many times on resampled versions of its own misses, carry-over and saturation included. A channel whose answer holds across the refits gets a clear verdict. One whose answer moves goes on the test list.

When should I not trust this?

When the held-back weeks miss by more than 10%. When two channels always run together. When spend chases demand, like search bids that rise in peak season. When something that moves sales is missing from the file. A mix model finds patterns in history. A lift test proves cause.
